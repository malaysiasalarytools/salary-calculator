What Is Tax Relief?

Tax relief is an amount that can be deducted when determining chargeable income. In Malaysia, eligible resident individuals may claim reliefs such as personal relief, EPF and insurance relief, lifestyle expenses, education fees, medical expenses and child-related reliefs.

Tax relief is not the same as a tax rebate. A relief reduces chargeable income before tax is calculated, while a rebate directly reduces tax payable.

Detailed Tax Relief Breakdown

The sections below explain common Malaysia tax relief categories in a practical way. For each category, check what normally qualifies, common examples, mistakes to avoid and documents to keep.

Lifestyle Relief

Lifestyle relief is one of the most commonly claimed personal tax reliefs because many employees already spend money on books, digital devices, internet subscriptions and other qualifying items.

The key point is that the expense must fall within the approved category and be supported by proper documents.

AreaDetails
What qualifiesQualifying lifestyle expenses may include books, journals, magazines, printed newspapers, personal computers, smartphones, tablets, internet subscriptions and approved self-development items, subject to current HASiL rules.
Common examplesA personal laptop, smartphone, home internet subscription or books for self-study may qualify when the conditions are met.
Common mistakesClaiming items for someone outside the permitted group, company-reimbursed expenses, non-qualifying items or expenses without proper evidence.
Documents requiredKeep tax invoices, receipts, online order confirmations, payment records and subscription bills showing the item, date and amount.

Medical Expenses Relief

Medical relief can be valuable for taxpayers who incur healthcare costs during the year, but not every medical bill automatically qualifies.

The claim depends on the type of treatment, who the treatment is for and whether the expense falls within an approved category or sub-limit.

AreaDetails
What qualifiesSpecified medical expenses may include serious illness treatment, fertility treatment, vaccination, dental examination / treatment and qualifying medical examinations, subject to the relevant limits and conditions.
Common examplesHospital bills for approved serious illness treatment, fertility treatment bills, vaccination receipts, dental treatment and qualifying health examinations.
Common mistakesClaiming general wellness products, supplements, cosmetic treatment or unsupported medical spending that does not fall within the approved category.
Documents requiredKeep hospital invoices, clinic receipts, medical reports where relevant, payment records and documents showing the patient and treatment date.

Education Fees Relief

Education fees relief is useful for taxpayers who continue studying, upgrading skills or taking approved professional courses. The course type and institution details matter.

AreaDetails
What qualifiesApproved self-education fees may qualify for specified academic, technical, professional, vocational, industrial, scientific or technology studies. Master's and PhD courses can qualify more broadly, and upskilling / self-enhancement is subject to its sub-limit.
Common examplesApproved degree, postgraduate, professional qualification, technical training and qualifying upskilling courses.
Common mistakesClaiming casual classes that do not qualify, another person's education fees, non-approved courses or expenses outside the permitted scope.
Documents requiredKeep official receipts, invoices, enrolment confirmations, institution details and payment records showing your name, course and payment year.

EPF, PRS, SOCSO and Insurance Relief

Contribution and insurance-related reliefs are important because they often appear directly in payroll records or annual statements.

AreaDetails
What qualifiesEmployee EPF contributions, approved PRS contributions, SOCSO contributions and selected life, family takaful, education or medical insurance premiums may qualify under their respective categories.
Common examplesEPF shown in payroll / statements, SOCSO payroll contributions, PRS statements and annual insurance premium statements.
Common mistakesMixing up insurance sub-limits, claiming employer-paid amounts incorrectly, double-claiming contributions or entering the wrong figures from statements.
Documents requiredKeep EA Form, EPF statement, SOCSO records, PRS statement, insurer statements and relevant payment receipts.

Child Relief and Family-Related Reliefs

Child and family reliefs depend on the child's age, education status, disability status, childcare arrangements and which spouse is claiming the relief.

AreaDetails
What qualifiesReliefs may apply for qualifying children below 18, children in full-time education, disabled children, registered childcare / kindergarten fees and SSPN net savings.
Common examplesRM2,000 for a qualifying child below 18, RM8,000 for a qualifying child in higher education, childcare fees and SSPN net savings subject to conditions.
Common mistakesBoth parents claiming the same full relief incorrectly, using the wrong education category, or claiming childcare / education items without supporting documents.
Documents requiredKeep birth certificates, education confirmation, university invoices, childcare receipts, SSPN statements and documents supporting the child's status.

How Tax Relief Reduces Your Tax

Tax relief reduces chargeable income before income tax is calculated. This means claiming RM2,500 of relief does not mean you automatically save RM2,500 of tax.

Approximate Tax Saving = Relief Amount × Marginal Tax Rate This is a simplified way to understand the effect when the entire relief falls within the same tax band.

Example: RM2,500 Lifestyle Relief

Assume a taxpayer's relevant marginal tax rate is 6% and the full RM2,500 lifestyle relief reduces income within that band.

Lifestyle relief claimedRM2,500
Illustrative marginal tax rate6%
Approximate tax savingRM150
Key point: Relief reduces taxable / chargeable income; a rebate reduces tax payable directly. Actual savings depend on the taxpayer's full tax calculation and the bands affected.

Can Tax Relief Reduce Monthly PCB?

Some eligible deductions and rebates can be taken into account for Monthly Tax Deduction when an employee submits Form TP1 to the employer.

TP1 can be submitted more than once in the current year where relevant. The employee normally submits the form to the employer without attaching the receipts, but must still keep the supporting receipts and documents.

SituationHow It Works
Claim through TP1Eligible deduction / rebate information may be reflected in the employer's PCB calculation.
No TP1 submittedQualifying relief may still be claimed in the annual tax return, subject to the tax rules.
Supporting documentsEmployees should keep receipts and supporting documents for 7 years from the year the claim is made.
Important: A lower PCB deduction does not itself prove that a relief is ultimately allowable. The taxpayer should still ensure the claim satisfies the tax rules and supporting evidence requirements.

How to Organise Tax Relief Documents

A simple way to reduce filing stress is to organise documents by category before filing season—for example, separate folders for lifestyle, medical, insurance / contributions and child / education claims.

Digital copies can be useful, but they should clearly show the date, amount, supplier and purpose of payment.

  • Check your EA Form and annual employment income
  • Review EPF, SOCSO, PRS and insurance statements
  • Collect receipts for lifestyle, medical and education claims
  • Check child relief, SSPN and childcare eligibility
  • Keep supporting documents for 7 years from the year of claim
  • Separate receipts by Year of Assessment to avoid claiming the wrong tax year

Common Tax Relief Mistakes

Some taxpayers overclaim tax relief because they assume every expense is deductible. Reliefs must fall within the approved category, meet the qualifying conditions and stay within the relevant limit.

🧾

Missing Receipts

Claims should be supported by receipts, statements or other relevant evidence.

📱

Wrong Category

An expense may look similar to a relief item but still fall outside the official qualifying rules.

👨‍👩‍👧

Shared Claims

Some family-related reliefs cannot simply be claimed in full by both spouses.

📅

Wrong Tax Year

Expenses are generally claimed based on the relevant Year of Assessment and payment period.

Tax Relief vs Tax Rebate vs Tax Deduction

These terms are related, but they do not work in the same way.

ItemHow It WorksSimple Example
Tax ReliefReduces chargeable income before tax is calculatedLifestyle, medical, EPF relief
Tax RebateReduces tax payable directlySpecific rebates allowed under tax rules
Tax DeductionReduces income based on approved deduction rulesApproved donations or other statutory deductions where applicable

Frequently Asked Questions

What is tax relief in Malaysia?

Tax relief is an amount that can reduce chargeable income for eligible taxpayers. Lower chargeable income may reduce the tax charged depending on the taxpayer's tax bands.

Is tax relief the same as a tax rebate?

No. Tax relief reduces chargeable income before tax is calculated, while a tax rebate reduces tax payable directly.

What is the individual relief for YA 2025?

The individual and dependent relatives relief is RM9,000 for YA 2025.

How much is the lifestyle relief for YA 2025?

The main lifestyle relief is restricted to RM2,500 for qualifying expenditure. A separate additional sports lifestyle relief of up to RM1,000 also applies to qualifying sports-related expenditure.

Can EPF contributions reduce chargeable income?

Qualifying EPF contributions may form part of the life insurance and EPF relief structure, subject to the relevant annual sub-limits and conditions.

Can medical expenses be claimed as tax relief?

Specified medical expenses may qualify, including certain serious illness treatment, fertility treatment, vaccination, dental treatment and medical examinations, subject to the applicable conditions and sub-limits.

Can tax relief reduce monthly PCB?

Eligible deductions and rebates may be reflected in PCB when the employee submits the relevant TP1 information to the employer. Qualifying relief can also be claimed during annual filing where applicable.

How long should I keep tax relief receipts?

Employees should keep receipts and supporting documents related to TP1 deductions for 7 years from the year the claim is made. Keeping tax filing support for the required retention period is important in case HASiL requests verification.

Does a RM2,500 tax relief save RM2,500 of tax?

No. A relief reduces chargeable income. The actual tax saving depends on the tax rate that applies to the income reduced by the relief.

Do tax relief limits change every year?

They can. Tax relief categories, limits and conditions may change by Year of Assessment, so always check the latest official HASiL guidance before filing.
