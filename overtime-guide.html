Learn how overtime pay works in Malaysia, including overtime rates, calculation examples, rest day work, public holiday work and common payroll questions.

Overtime Quick Summary

Statutory overtime rules under the Employment Act 1955 depend on the employee category, wages and the day on which the extra work is performed.

1.5×Minimum hourly rate for overtime beyond normal hours on an ordinary working day
2.0×Rate for hours beyond normal hours when working on a rest day
3.0×Rate for overtime hours beyond normal hours on a paid public holiday
45 HoursGeneral maximum normal working hours per week under the Act
Important: Rest-day and public-holiday pay have their own statutory payment rules. It is not correct to treat every hour worked on a rest day as simply 2×, or every hour on a public holiday as simply 3×.

What Is Overtime Pay?

Under the Employment Act 1955, overtime generally means work performed beyond the employee's normal hours of work per day. For employees entitled to statutory overtime, ordinary-day overtime must be paid at not less than 1.5 times the hourly rate of pay.

Work on a rest day and work on a paid public holiday are governed by separate provisions, so their calculations differ from ordinary-day overtime.

Who Is Entitled to Statutory Overtime Pay?

The Employment Act now generally covers employees regardless of wage level, but the statutory overtime provisions do not apply in exactly the same way to everyone.

Employee CategoryGeneral Statutory OT Position
Employee earning RM4,000 or less per monthGenerally covered by the statutory overtime, rest-day and paid-holiday payment provisions.
Employee earning above RM4,000 per monthGenerally excluded from statutory overtime / rest-day / paid-holiday payment provisions unless the employee falls within a protected First Schedule category.
Manual labour and certain specified categoriesMay remain covered regardless of the RM4,000 wage threshold, subject to the First Schedule.
Employee not statutorily entitledMay still receive overtime if the employment contract, collective agreement or company policy provides for it.
Practical check: Do not look at salary alone. Check the First Schedule, the employee's actual duties and the employment contract before deciding whether statutory overtime applies.

Normal Working Hours in Malaysia

The Employment Act generally limits normal working hours to 45 hours per week, excluding rest intervals where the employee is free to use the time and movement.

The Act also regulates daily working arrangements, rest breaks and spread-over periods. The actual normal hours per day should be stated or agreed in the contract of service and used when determining when overtime begins.

Example: A company may arrange a 45-hour week over five or six working days. Overtime starts after the employee's normal daily hours, subject to the applicable statutory rules.

How Ordinary Rate of Pay and Hourly Rate Are Calculated

For a monthly-rated employee, statutory overtime commonly starts with the ordinary rate of pay (ORP). Under the Act, the monthly ordinary rate is generally based on monthly wages divided by 26.

Ordinary Rate of Pay (ORP) = Monthly Wages ÷ 26 For a monthly-rated employee, subject to the statutory definition of wages.
Hourly Rate of Pay (HRP) = ORP ÷ Normal Hours of Work Per Day This hourly rate is then used for statutory overtime calculations.
Do not simply use salary ÷ 30 ÷ 8 unless that method is specifically appropriate to the payment being calculated. Statutory overtime under the Employment Act uses the statutory ORP / HRP framework.

Overtime on an Ordinary Working Day

For an eligible employee who works beyond normal hours on an ordinary working day, the overtime rate must be at least 1.5 times the hourly rate of pay.

Ordinary-Day OT = HRP × 1.5 × Overtime Hours Applies to overtime hours beyond the employee's normal working hours.

Work on a Rest Day

Rest-day pay is more detailed than simply “2×”. For a monthly- or weekly-rated employee, payment depends on how many hours are worked before exceeding the normal daily hours.

Hours Worked on Rest DayStatutory Payment for Monthly / Weekly Rated Employee
Not more than half the normal daily hoursWages equivalent to half the ordinary rate of pay for that day.
More than half but not more than normal daily hoursWages equivalent to one day's ordinary rate of pay.
Hours exceeding normal daily hoursThose excess hours are paid at not less than 2× the hourly rate of pay.
Why this matters: “Rest day = 2×” is only a shortcut for overtime hours beyond normal daily hours, not the complete rest-day payment rule.

Work on a Paid Public Holiday

An eligible employee who works on a paid public holiday is generally entitled to holiday pay plus additional statutory payment for the work performed. Overtime beyond normal hours on that holiday is paid at a higher hourly multiplier.

Public Holiday WorkGeneral Statutory Treatment
Work within normal hoursIn addition to holiday pay, the employee is generally entitled to two days' wages at the ordinary rate of pay.
Hours beyond normal hoursOvertime hours are paid at not less than 3× the hourly rate of pay.
Important: “Public holiday = 3×” refers to overtime hours beyond normal hours. The statutory payment for working normal hours on the holiday is structured differently.

Overtime Calculation Example

Assume a monthly-rated eligible employee earns RM3,000 and normally works 8 hours per day.

Monthly wagesRM3,000
ORPRM3,000 ÷ 26 = RM115.38
HRPRM115.38 ÷ 8 = RM14.42
4 hours ordinary-day OTRM14.42 × 1.5 × 4 = RM86.52
Figures are rounded for illustration. Payroll systems may apply statutory rounding and wage-component rules differently.

Does Overtime Affect EPF, SOCSO, EIS and PCB?

Overtime can affect payroll deductions differently because each statutory scheme has its own definition of wages or remuneration.

Payroll ItemOvertime TreatmentPractical Note
EPFGenerally not subject to EPFKWSP excludes overtime payment from wages liable for EPF contribution.
SOCSOGenerally subject to SOCSOOvertime is included in PERKESO's wage definition.
EISGenerally subject to EISOvertime forms part of wages under the EIS rules.
PCBMay affect PCBTaxable employment remuneration can affect the monthly MTD calculation.
Useful payroll distinction: Overtime is one of the clearest examples of why EPF, SOCSO and EIS should not be calculated using identical wage components.

Overtime Pay vs Replacement Leave

Some companies offer time off or replacement leave for additional work. However, company policy cannot simply remove a statutory overtime entitlement where the Employment Act requires overtime payment.

For employees outside statutory overtime provisions, contractual time-off arrangements may depend more heavily on the employment contract, company policy or collective agreement.

Common Overtime Payroll Mistakes

  • Assuming every employee is statutorily entitled to overtime without checking the First Schedule.
  • Using a simple salary ÷ 30 ÷ 8 hourly-rate formula instead of the statutory ORP / HRP method.
  • Treating all rest-day hours as 2×.
  • Treating all public-holiday hours as 3×.
  • Using 48 hours instead of the current 45-hour general weekly working-hours limit.
  • Failing to record or approve overtime hours properly.
  • Applying EPF, SOCSO and EIS to overtime as though all three use the same wage definition.
  • Replacing statutory overtime pay with time off without checking whether that is legally permissible.

Employee Overtime Checklist

  • Check whether statutory overtime provisions apply to your employee category.
  • Confirm your normal daily and weekly working hours.
  • Keep attendance, clock-in / clock-out and overtime approval records.
  • Check whether the overtime was on an ordinary day, rest day or paid public holiday.
  • Recalculate ORP and HRP if you want to verify the payslip amount.
  • Check SOCSO, EIS, EPF and PCB treatment separately.
  • Raise payroll differences promptly with HR / payroll.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the overtime rate on an ordinary working day?

For an employee entitled to statutory overtime, work beyond normal hours on an ordinary working day must generally be paid at not less than 1.5 times the hourly rate of pay.

Are employees earning above RM4,000 entitled to statutory overtime?

Employees earning more than RM4,000 per month are generally excluded from the statutory overtime, rest-day and paid-holiday payment provisions unless they fall within specified protected categories such as certain manual employees under the First Schedule.

What is the current normal working-hours limit in Malaysia?

The Employment Act generally provides for a maximum of 45 normal working hours per week, excluding qualifying rest intervals.

How is the hourly rate for statutory overtime calculated?

For a monthly-rated employee, the ordinary rate is generally monthly wages divided by 26, and the hourly rate is the ordinary rate divided by normal hours of work per day.

Is all rest-day work paid at 2×?

No. Monthly- and weekly-rated employees have half-day and full-day rest-day payment rules. The 2× hourly rate applies to hours worked beyond normal daily hours.

Is all public-holiday work paid at 3×?

No. Work within normal hours on a paid public holiday has its own additional payment rule. The 3× hourly rate applies to overtime hours beyond normal daily hours on that holiday.

Is overtime subject to EPF?

Overtime payment is generally excluded from wages liable for EPF contribution.

Is overtime subject to SOCSO and EIS?

Overtime is generally included in the wage definitions used for SOCSO and EIS contribution purposes.

Can an employer require overtime approval?

Employers may use reasonable approval and record procedures. However, whether overtime is payable ultimately depends on the actual statutory and contractual entitlement, not merely the label used internally.

Can statutory overtime be replaced by time off?

An employer should not assume replacement leave can remove a statutory overtime entitlement. Where statutory overtime applies, the Employment Act payment requirements should be checked.
