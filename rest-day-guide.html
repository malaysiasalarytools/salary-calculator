Malaysia Rest Day Guide 2026

Learn how rest day works in Malaysia, including weekly rest, working on rest day, rest day pay, shift work, public holiday differences and common employment questions.

Rest Day Quick Summary

Sections 59 and 60 of the Employment Act 1955 set the main rules on weekly rest days and payment for employees who work on a statutory rest day.

1 Whole DayMinimum weekly rest day for an employee under section 59
30 HoursContinuous period that may constitute a rest day for a shift worker
½ / 1 DayMonthly / weekly employee payment for work up to normal hours
2×Minimum hourly rate for hours beyond normal daily hours on a rest day
Important: “Rest day work = 2×” is not the complete rule. For monthly- and weekly-rated employees, work up to normal daily hours follows the statutory half-day / one-day payment structure; the 2× hourly rate applies only to hours beyond normal daily hours.

What Is a Rest Day?

A rest day is the statutory weekly day of rest under section 59 of the Employment Act 1955. It is different from annual leave, a paid public holiday and an ordinary non-working day created by a five-day or rotating work schedule.

The distinction matters because section 60 uses the statutory rest-day classification when determining special payment for employees who work on that day.

Weekly Rest Day Entitlement in Malaysia

Section 59 provides that every employee must be allowed one whole rest day in each week, with the employer determining the day from time to time.

Rest-Day RuleStatutory Position
Minimum weekly restOne whole day in each week.
Who chooses the day?The employer may determine the rest day, subject to the Act and the employee's work arrangement.
Must it be Sunday?No. The rest day can be another day, especially for shift, retail, hospitality and roster-based work.
Can roster rotate?Yes. The employer may appoint different rest days, provided the statutory requirements are followed.
Scope note: This guide focuses on the Employment Act 1955 framework applicable in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan. Sabah and Sarawak have separate labour legislation.

What If You Have Two Days Off Every Week?

This is especially important for employees on a five-day workweek. Section 59 says that where an employee is allowed more than one rest day in a week, the last of those rest days is treated as the statutory rest day for Part XII.

Example: Saturday and Sunday Off

If an employee is given Saturday and Sunday as two weekly rest days, Sunday — being the last of those rest days — is generally the statutory rest day for Part XII purposes.

This means payroll should not automatically classify Saturday and Sunday identically when applying the statutory rest-day payment provisions.

Very useful payroll distinction: A normal “off day” and the statutory “rest day” can be different even though both are days the employee does not normally work.

Rest-Day Roster and Shift Workers

Where rest days vary, section 59 requires the employer to prepare a roster before the beginning of the month showing the employee's appointed rest days. If every employee has the same fixed rest day, the employer may display a notice instead.

For shift workers, section 59 specifically provides that a continuous period of at least 30 hours may constitute a rest day, rather than requiring a midnight-to-midnight calendar day.

Can an Employer Require You to Work on a Rest Day?

Section 60 generally states that an employee should not be compelled to work on a rest day unless the work, by its nature, must be carried on continuously or continually by two or more shifts.

Section 60A(2) also allows rest-day work in specified exceptional situations such as accidents, urgent machinery work, unforeseeable interruptions, essential community work and certain essential services.

Practical point: “Business is busy” should not automatically be treated as an unlimited right to compel statutory rest-day work. The legal circumstances and work arrangement still matter.

Rest-Day Pay for Monthly- or Weekly-Rated Employees

For an employee entitled to section 60 rest-day payment and paid on a monthly or weekly basis, the statutory amount depends on how long the employee works on the rest day.

Hours Worked on Rest DayMonthly / Weekly Employee Payment
Not more than half normal daily hoursWages equivalent to ½ of the ordinary rate of pay (ORP) for that day.
More than half but not more than normal daily hours1 day's wages at ORP.
Hours beyond normal daily hoursEach excess hour is paid at not less than 2× the hourly rate of pay (HRP).
This is why “rest day = 2×” is wrong: 2× applies to the excess hours beyond normal daily hours, not automatically to the first hours worked on the rest day.

Example: Monthly Employee Works on a Rest Day

Assume a qualifying monthly-rated employee earns RM3,000 and normally works 8 hours per day.

Monthly wagesRM3,000
ORPRM3,000 ÷ 26 = RM115.38
HRPRM115.38 ÷ 8 = RM14.42
Works 4 hours or less½ ORP = RM57.69
Works more than 4 but up to 8 hours1 ORP = RM115.38
Works 2 extra hours after completing 8 hoursExtra portion: RM14.42 × 2 × 2 = RM57.68

If the employee works 10 hours in total, the statutory calculation combines the full normal-hours rest-day payment with the separate 2× HRP payment for the two excess hours.

Rest-Day Pay for Daily- or Hourly-Rated Employees

The first part of the rest-day calculation is different for daily, hourly or similar-rated employees.

Hours Worked on Rest DayDaily / Hourly Employee Payment
Not more than half normal daily hours1 day's wages at ORP.
More than half but not more than normal daily hours2 days' wages at ORP.
Hours beyond normal daily hoursEach excess hour is paid at not less than 2× HRP.
Payroll warning: Do not use the monthly-rated employee table for a daily- or hourly-rated employee. Section 60 gives them different payment amounts for work up to normal daily hours.

Who Is Entitled to Statutory Rest-Day Work Pay?

The Employment Act generally covers private-sector employees, but the First Schedule excludes certain higher-paid employees from the statutory payment provisions for rest-day work.

Employee CategoryGeneral Statutory Rest-Day Pay Position
Employee earning RM4,000 or less per monthGenerally covered by section 60 payment provisions.
Employee earning above RM4,000 per monthGenerally excluded from statutory rest-day payment claims unless within a protected First Schedule category.
Manual employee and certain specified categoriesMay remain covered regardless of the RM4,000 wage threshold.
Employee outside section 60 payment entitlementMay still receive contractual rest-day / overtime compensation if the employment contract or company policy provides it.
Important distinction: An employee above RM4,000 is not thereby denied a weekly rest day. The wage-based exclusion concerns specific Part XII payment provisions such as statutory rest-day work pay.

How ORP and Hourly Rate Are Calculated

For a monthly-rated employee, section 60I generally calculates the ordinary rate of pay using monthly wages divided by 26.

ORP = Monthly Wages ÷ 26 For monthly-rated employees, subject to the statutory definition of wages.
HRP = ORP ÷ Normal Hours of Work Per Day Used for the 2× excess-hour calculation on a rest day.

An employer may use another method if it does not result in a rate lower than the statutory rate provided by section 60I.

What If a Public Holiday Falls on a Rest Day?

Rest day and public holiday rules interact. Under section 60D, when an applicable paid public holiday falls on the employee's rest day, the immediately following working day becomes a paid holiday in substitution.

Why this matters: First identify the employee's actual statutory rest day. In a two-day weekend, the second / last rest day may be the statutory rest day for Part XII purposes.

Common Rest Day Examples

Example 1: Five-Day Office Week

An employee does not work on Saturday or Sunday. If both are treated as rest days, the last one — Sunday — is the statutory rest day for Part XII purposes.

Example 2: Rotating Retail Rest Day

A retail employee works weekends and has Wednesday as the rostered rest day. Wednesday, not Sunday, is the day payroll should use when assessing statutory rest-day work.

Example 3: Monthly Employee Works Half a Day

A qualifying monthly-rated employee works no more than half of normal daily hours on the statutory rest day. Section 60 provides payment equivalent to half the ordinary rate of pay for that day.

Example 4: Employee Works Beyond Normal Hours

After the employee has worked beyond normal daily hours on the rest day, each excess hour is paid at not less than 2× the hourly rate of pay where section 60 applies.

Common Rest Day Mistakes

  • Assuming every non-working day is the statutory rest day.
  • Ignoring the “last rest day” rule when employees have two or more weekly days off.
  • Assuming Sunday must always be the rest day.
  • Not checking the roster for shift or rotating-schedule employees.
  • Thinking every hour worked on a rest day is automatically paid at 2×.
  • Using the monthly / weekly rest-day payment table for daily / hourly employees.
  • Ignoring the RM4,000 / First Schedule rules for statutory rest-day pay.
  • Using salary ÷ 30 instead of the statutory ORP framework for section 60 calculations.
  • Confusing a public holiday falling on a rest day with an ordinary off-day situation.
  • Failing to keep clear attendance, roster and approval records.

Employee Rest Day Checklist

  • Identify your official statutory rest day from the roster or workplace notice.
  • If you have two days off, check which is the last rest day for Part XII purposes.
  • Confirm whether you are monthly / weekly or daily / hourly rated.
  • Check whether section 60 statutory payment applies to your employee category.
  • Record actual hours worked on the rest day.
  • Separate normal rest-day hours from hours beyond normal daily hours.
  • Recalculate ORP / HRP if the payslip amount looks wrong.
  • Check whether a public holiday also affects the day.
  • Keep roster and payroll records if you need to raise a dispute.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many rest days are employees entitled to in Malaysia?

Section 59 generally requires every employee to be allowed one whole rest day in each week.

Does a rest day have to be Sunday?

No. The employer may determine the weekly rest day. Shift and roster employees commonly have rest days on other days of the week.

If I have Saturday and Sunday off, which one is the statutory rest day?

Where an employee is allowed more than one rest day in a week, section 59 states that the last of those rest days is the rest day for Part XII purposes. In a Saturday-Sunday example, that would generally be Sunday.

How long is a rest day for a shift worker?

For a shift worker, a continuous period of at least 30 hours may constitute a rest day under section 59.

Can my employer require me to work on my rest day?

Section 60 generally restricts compulsory rest-day work, with exceptions for continuous / shift-based work and specified circumstances under section 60A(2).

Is all rest-day work paid at 2×?

No. For monthly- and weekly-rated employees, work up to normal daily hours follows half-ORP / one-ORP rules. The 2× HRP rate applies to hours beyond normal daily hours.

How is rest-day pay calculated for a monthly employee?

If work does not exceed half normal daily hours, payment is half ORP. If work exceeds half but not normal daily hours, payment is one ORP. Excess hours beyond normal daily hours are paid at not less than 2× HRP.

Are daily-rated employees paid the same way?

No. Daily / hourly employees receive one day's ORP for work up to half normal hours and two days' ORP for more than half up to normal hours, with excess hours paid at not less than 2× HRP.

Are employees earning above RM4,000 entitled to statutory rest-day work pay?

Employees earning above RM4,000 per month are generally excluded from statutory rest-day payment claims unless they fall within protected categories such as certain manual employees under the First Schedule.

What happens if a public holiday falls on my rest day?

Under section 60D, the immediately following working day generally becomes a paid substitute holiday where an applicable public holiday falls on the statutory rest day.

How is ORP calculated for a monthly-rated employee?

Section 60I generally calculates the ordinary rate of pay as monthly wages divided by 26, and HRP is ORP divided by normal daily hours.
