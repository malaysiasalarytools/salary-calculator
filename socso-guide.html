What Is SOCSO?

SOCSO stands for Social Security Organisation, also known as PERKESO in Malaysia. It provides protection to eligible employees in cases such as employment injury, occupational disease, invalidity and death.

Unlike EPF, which focuses mainly on retirement savings, SOCSO is designed as a social protection scheme. It helps employees and their families receive financial support when covered events occur.

Who Needs to Contribute to SOCSO?

SOCSO generally covers eligible employees working under a contract of service or apprenticeship in Malaysia. This includes many private-sector employees as well as eligible contract or temporary employees.

Permanent Federal and State Government employees, the self-employed, sole proprietors and partners in a partnership are among the categories that may fall outside normal Employees' Social Security Act coverage. Employee category, age and employment status should therefore be checked before payroll is processed.

Payroll reminder: Employers should confirm employee eligibility and use the latest PERKESO contribution schedule when preparing actual monthly contributions.

SOCSO First Category vs Second Category

SOCSO contributions under the Employees' Social Security Act are commonly divided into two contribution categories. The applicable category depends mainly on the employee's age and contribution history.

CategoryWho It Commonly Applies ToContribution Structure
First CategoryEmployees below age 60, subject to PERKESO rules and exceptionsEmployer and employee contribute for Employment Injury and Invalidity protection.
Second CategoryEmployees aged 60 and above, and certain employees with no previous contribution historyEmployer-only contribution for Employment Injury protection.
Under the commonly applicable First Category, the headline rates are 1.75% employer and 0.5% employee. Actual statutory amounts should still follow the official wage-bracket contribution schedule.

Which Payments Are Subject to SOCSO?

SOCSO uses a definition of wages that includes many forms of cash remuneration paid to employees. This is different from EPF treatment for some items, so payroll should not assume that the same salary component rules apply to every statutory contribution.

Commission / Service ChargeYesCommission and service charges are included.

Retrenchment / Retirement GratuityNoGratuity and dismissal or retrenchment payments are generally excluded.
Annual BonusNoAnnual bonus is listed by PERKESO as not forming part of wages for contribution purposes.
Key difference from EPF: Overtime is generally subject to SOCSO even though overtime payment is not liable for EPF contribution.

How SOCSO Contributions Are Calculated

SOCSO is not normally calculated by simply multiplying salary by one fixed percentage. Instead, the employee's monthly wages are matched to the applicable wage bracket in the official PERKESO contribution schedule.

SOCSO Contribution = Statutory Amount for the Applicable Wage BracketEmployee and employer contributions should be taken from the latest PERKESO contribution schedule.

Example: RM4,000 Monthly Salary

For a First Category employee with monthly wages of RM4,000, the contribution at this reference point is:

What Does SOCSO Cover?

SOCSO is designed to provide social security protection rather than personal savings. Depending on the scheme, eligibility and circumstances, protection may include medical treatment and cash benefits.

Employment Injury

Protection for eligible accidents or occupational injuries arising from employment.

Invalidity

Support may be available when an insured employee becomes permanently incapable of earning due to invalidity.

Medical Benefits

Eligible employment injury cases may receive medical treatment through PERKESO arrangements.

Dependants' Benefits

Eligible dependants may receive financial support when a covered insured person dies.

Employee vs Employer SOCSO Contribution

In Malaysia payroll, SOCSO contributions involve separate employee and employer portions. The employee contribution is deducted from salary, while the employer contribution is paid by the employer and does not reduce the employee's take-home pay.

SOCSO Salary Ceiling and Wage Brackets

SOCSO contributions are determined using wage brackets rather than a simple flat calculation. Two employees with salaries in the same statutory wage band may therefore have the same scheduled contribution amount.

The current contribution ceiling is RM6,000 per month. For wages above the ceiling, the contribution is generally capped at the amount applicable to RM6,000.

How SOCSO Affects Monthly Take-Home Pay

SOCSO reduces take-home pay by the employee contribution amount only. The employer's SOCSO portion is not deducted from the employee's gross salary.

SOCSO Contribution Deadline

SOCSO contributions for a particular month must generally be paid no later than the 15th day of the following month.

Employer Responsibilities
  • Identify eligible employees
  • Use the correct wage bracket
  • Deduct the employee share correctly
  • Pay the employer share and submit on time
Late payment: PERKESO states that interest on late contribution payment may be imposed at 6% per annum for late contributions.

Common Payroll Mistakes With SOCSO

A common mistake is treating SOCSO as a simple percentage of salary instead of using the statutory contribution schedule and wage bracket. Another common mistake is assuming EPF and SOCSO use the same definition of contributable wages.

Employers should also be careful when processing overtime, allowances, bonuses, new employees, salary changes and employees who move into a different age category.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SOCSO contribution mandatory for employees in Malaysia?

SOCSO is generally mandatory for eligible employees covered under the Employees' Social Security Act. Employers are responsible for registering eligible employees and making the required monthly contributions.

How much SOCSO does an employee contribute every month?

For many First Category employees below age 60, the headline employee rate is 0.5% of wages, but the actual payroll amount should be taken from the official PERKESO wage-bracket contribution schedule.

How much SOCSO does an employer contribute?

For many First Category employees, the headline employer rate is 1.75% of wages. The actual statutory amount depends on the applicable wage bracket in the PERKESO contribution schedule.

Is SOCSO calculated on basic salary or gross salary?

SOCSO is based on wages liable for contribution. Salary, overtime, commissions, service charges, leave pay and various allowances may form part of wages, while items such as travelling claims and annual bonus are generally excluded.

Is overtime subject to SOCSO?

Yes. PERKESO includes overtime payments in the definition of wages for SOCSO contribution purposes.

Is annual bonus subject to SOCSO?

No. PERKESO lists annual bonus among payments that are not treated as wages for SOCSO contribution purposes.

What is the SOCSO salary ceiling?

The current monthly wage ceiling for contribution purposes is RM6,000. Wages above the ceiling are generally subject to the maximum scheduled contribution amount.

Does SOCSO affect take-home pay?

Yes. The employee portion is deducted from monthly salary. The employer portion is paid separately by the employer and does not reduce take-home pay.

When must employers pay SOCSO contributions?

Contributions for a month must generally be paid no later than the 15th day of the following month.

Is SOCSO the same as EIS?

No. SOCSO provides social security protection such as employment injury and invalidity protection, while EIS focuses on employment insurance and support for eligible employees who lose their jobs.
